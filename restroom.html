The question

Splitting a restroom into equal floor areas sounds fair. But equal space does not necessarily create equal capacity or equal waits. Different fixtures, visit durations, and arrival patterns change the outcome.

What happens when we design around waiting time, rather than a line down the middle of the floor?

What I built

A browser-based simulator that lets people explore six restroom layouts, change arrival rates, and watch people move through the space. The project combines a 2D view, a 3D environment, and an illustrated explanation of the design tradeoffs.

The interface exposes queue length, fixture usage, throughput, and average wait times. That makes the model something a visitor can investigate, rather than a single result they have to accept.

Three decisions worth examining

  • Equal allocation or equal outcomes? Equal floor area is a starting condition, not a guarantee of equal waiting time.
  • Fairness or total throughput? A layout that serves more people overall may still distribute the waiting unevenly.
  • Separate capacity or shared capacity? Pooling stalls changes who can use the available fixtures when demand fluctuates.

How to explore it

Choose “Skip to simulation,” select a layout, and press play. Try Equal Space first, then compare it with Mixed Access while keeping the arrival rate the same. Use the controls to rotate the model and inspect how people move. The simulator works best with room for the model and its controls on a desktop screen.

What the visual story does—and doesn’t—show

The Results section is an illustrated explanation; it is not a replay of the current live run. Its displayed example compares women’s waits of 88 and 44 seconds, and men’s waits of 32 and 38 seconds, for two layouts. Those values describe that example, not a universal prediction or measured building outcome.

A defensible comparison needs repeated runs under matched assumptions, reported variability, and sensitivity checks across demand levels and service times. A single animated run is useful for exploring behavior, but cannot establish which design is always best.

What I’d develop next

A reproducible comparison mode with controlled random seeds, repeated trials, and uncertainty intervals. I’d also make the 3D controls more comfortable on small screens, and bring accessibility, privacy, and real-world space constraints into the design discussion.
